The Circassians of the North Caucasus were among the oldest peoples of Europe, and one of its last great pagan ones. They built no churches and carved no idols of their high god. They worshipped a supreme creator they called Tha, maker of the universe, whose law, the Khabze, shaped every part of life.  Beneath Tha stood a whole pantheon of nature. Shible, god of thunder and lightning. Tlepsh, the divine blacksmith who forged the first swords and healed the wounded. Mezitha, the forest goddess who ruled the wild beasts and the hunt. And gods of the water, the harvest, the horses, and the mountains.

Their temples were living trees. The Circassians worshipped in untouched sacred groves of ancient oak, linden, and plane, never built on, never cut. To fell one was believed to bring ruin on the entire tribe. This was a faith that asked you to guard the forest, not conquer it. Their holy places were untouched groves of oak and linden. Russian authorities destroyed Oak trees to hurt Circassiaan sentiments.
