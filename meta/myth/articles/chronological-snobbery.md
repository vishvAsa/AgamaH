What Pinker is doing has a name. C.S. Lewis called it chronological snobbery - the assumption that the past matters mainly as a measure of our own ascent. And obviously our morals are better, and with 3,000 years of hindsight they'd better be. But that's an answer to a question nobody's asking of Homer. Nobody opens the Odyssey to check the ethics. Reading Homer to feel superior to him is like visiting a cathedral to admire your own flashlight. The comparison isn't the gift. The gift is what hasn't changed

Hemingway said "all you have to do is write one true sentence. write the truest sentence that you know." That's what these stories are, they contain true sentences & lessons that stayed true. Courage is still courage. Grief is still grief. Loyalty, the longing for home, the love between parent and child, the pull between glory and a long quiet life... none of it has been upgraded or outgrown. Penelope's fidelity isn't a primitive draft of a feeling we've since improved. It's permanent. This is why great art can survive 3,000 years

Stories don't only warn us what not to be, they hold up what's worth being. They teach courage, honor, endurance, how to reach for the heroic. That's the permanent stuff of being human, and it's what chronological snobbery can't see
