## veda
Whereas Jainam would formulate the idea of a Tīrthaṅkara much later—where this exalted soul metaphorically constructs a “Tīrtha”/ford for others to cross the ocean of Saṁsāra, several centuries prior to the rise of the Nāstikas, the very opening of the sixth kāṇḍa of the Taittirīya-Saṁhitā already envisions the Yajamāna becoming the Tīrtha itself--i.e. through the Yajamāna, his peers (samāna-s) cross. See attached screenshots of the original from the Veda as well as Bhaṭṭabhāskara's commentary on this sentence, which is as follows:

> फलं चाह—तीर्थमेवेति । समानानां सजातानां तीर्थमेव भवति यजमानः । यथा तत् स्नानपानावगाहादिना लोकस्योपकारकं भवति, एवमसौ समानानां बहुप्रकारमुपजीव्यो भवति ॥

> phalaṃ cāha—tīrthameveti | samānānāṃ sajātānāṃ tīrthameva bhavati yajamānaḥ | yathā tat snānapānāvagāhādinā lokasyopakārakaṃ bhavati, evamasau samānānāṃ bahuprakāramupajīvyo bhavati ||

> "And he states the result [by saying], 'Verily, a ford (tīrtha) [he becomes]...'
>
> The Yajamāna becomes a ford (tīrtha) to his peers, [to his] kinsmen. Just as that [ford] becomes beneficial to people through bathing, drinking, immersing, and so forth, in the same way, this person [the patron] becomes a source of livelihood and sustenance in many ways to his peers."

The ritual bath, where the Yajamāna-Dīkṣita becomes a Tīrtha for his peers, prefigures the fuller and even more profound revelations of Īśvara, which were to come. 

At a most literal level, “samānāḥ” can be said to denote the Yajamāna’s kinsmen as the commentator notes. 

## samānāḥ - all
However, with the Upaniṣads and other Śāstras (such as the Mahābhārata; particularly the Gītā) establishing the divinity inherent in every soul (though different schools frame this same point in their own unique ways), the ambit of samānāḥ broadens to encompass all creatures, which are all sajātāḥ in the sense that they are all born into the cosmos made of māyā/prakṛti. 

## Rantideva
Thus we see in the Bhāgavata the story of Rantideva who wishes to become a Tīrtha for others—to remain in perpetuity taking upon, and therefore easing, the sufferings of all embodied, sentient beings:

> न कामयेऽहं गतिमीश्वरात् पराम्  
> अष्टर्द्धियुक्ताम् अपुनर्भवं वा ।
आर्तिं प्रपद्येऽखिलदेहभाजाम्  
> अन्तःस्थितो येन भवन्त्यदुःखाः ॥ १२ ॥

na kāmaye ’haṁ gatim īśvarāt parām
aṣṭarddhi-yuktām apunar-bhavaṁ vā
ārtiṁ prapadye ’khila-deha-bhājām
antaḥ-sthito yena bhavanty aduḥkhāḥ

> “I do not desire from Īśvara the highest end consisting of the [possession of the] eight siddhi-s or even repeated existence (continuing rebirth). I seek the sufferings of all embodied creatures, staying among them, by which they may become free from sorrows.”

## Lińgapurāņa
Thus, we see in the Lińgapurāņa the essence of Așțamūrti-Upāsana (the worship of the 8 forms of Śiva consisting of the Pañcabhūtas, Sūrya, Candra & the Yajamāna/Ātmā): To give abhaya and offer help to any sentient being in need Is the highest form of worship:

> abhayaṃ yat pradattaṃ syādaṅgino vasya kasyacit //
ārādhanaṃ kṛtaṃ tasmādaṣṭamūrterna saṃśayaḥ /
sarvopakārakaraṇaṃ pradānamabhayasya ca //
ārādhanaṃ tu devasya aṣṭamūrterna saṃśayaḥ /
sarvopakārakaraṇaṃ sarvānugraha eva ca //
tadarcanaṃ paraṃ prāhur aṣṭamūrter munīśvarāḥ /
anugrahaṇamanyeṣāṃ vidhātavyaṃ tvayaṅginām //
sarvābhayapradānaṃ ca śivārādhanam icchatā //

> “Whatever freedom from fear is granted to any embodied being—
that indeed is worship rendered unto the Eight-formed Lord, without doubt.

> Therefore, rendering help unto all, and granting freedom from fear—
this indeed is worship of the God of the eight forms, beyond doubt.

Doing beneficial [acts of service] unto all and showing grace toward all beings—
this is certainly the worship of the Lord in His eightfold manifestation.

The great sages declare that this is the supreme worship of the Eight-formed One:
one ought to bestow grace upon other embodied beings.

And the granting of freedom from fear unto all—
that is the worship of Śiva for one who desires [to worship Him]”
