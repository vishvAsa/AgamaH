Svabhāva

A firm belief in Svabhāva immensely helps one do dharma without expectation of phala. If it is your svabhāva to do dharma (help Dhārmika causes, spread knowledge, teach as much as your strength allows, etc), do it to the extent you are allowed and you are able to do so.

I think some people are genuinely meant for certain sampradāyas (or at most a combination: śrauta/smārta + 1 Āgamika) and sampradāyas are doing a huge disservice to them by not being more proactive in taking in those whose spiritual trajectory depends on that particular Sampradāya

To avoid

Don’t indulge in pointless discourse. It is a huge drain on energy and mental peace.

Those who have a svabhāva for higher things (in various degrees, regardless of their background) will appreciate what you are doing and benefit from your work or even join in (if they have the strength and ability to do so).

Those who do not have—they will mostly ignore. If they do not and go apeshit, respond once or twice (to defend yourself from slander) and let it go.

All available energy and time should be expended in building communities of common intent and aim—be it virtual or real.

Proselytization target

Those in a position to “proselytize”— do that internally, aiming at those who have a taste for the higher things but have not had an opportunity to discover their own potential. Don’t waste your time on the hateful who are Hindus only in name.

Rather than serving as a basis for divisions, the Sampradāyas can actually serve as a basis for a greater commitment to Hindudharma.

Specific sampradāyas should try to make themselves more accessible and open. Each of the sat-sampradāyas has a unique way of anchoring Hindus to that larger compact/covenant called Hindudharma.

Focus

Young people of Sampradāyas should do whatever they can in this regard, in line with their respective abilities.

Every Sampradāya is a potential lifelong anchor for those with the Svabhāva for that Sampradāya. Instead of wasting their lives on “hodgepodge Hinduism” or “Hinduismus Genericus”, they can make the best out of their lives by committing to a codified, rich Sampradāya with a structured path and discipline.

General Proselytization

Source: TW

There is more or less a common ground for all mainstream Sampradāyas: Karma/Punarjanma, Infinite cycles of Sṛṣṭi/Sam̐hāra, Īśvara & Divine authority of Veda, even though the last two vary due to theological nuances.

But it’s certainly more than enough for a creedal unity. But, this will become a real common ground only if it proliferates among an overwhelming majority of Hindus & this will happen only if Sampradāyas proselytise sufficiently among “general Hindus”.

By this, I don’t mean formal initiations. I mean, through writings & simple public discourses in temples in their respective turfs. I think it is already happening.

Most H who listen to Śaiva, Śākta, Advaita, Rāmānuja, Mādhva, Gauḍiya, Rāmānandi, Nimbarka, Puṣṭimārga, etc already share these basic tenets. But the audience for Satsampradāya content is still too low. They need to invest more effort in this.

Say if a Sampradāya YT channel uploads 100 videos, about 65 can focus on their own Sampradāya Viṣayas but remaining 35 must be introductory in nature & focus on the common ground outlined above, expressly pointing their across-the-board canonical/creedal nature.

A tentative list of common ground subjects:

  1. Karma/Punarjanma
  2. Anādyananta Sṛṣti-Sam̐hāra
  3. Īśvara
  4. Veda’s Prāmāṇyam
  5. Pitṛkarma
  6. Basic Śauca
  7. Basic Varṇāśrama
  8. Basic Strīdharma
  9. General Sam̐skāras
  10. Importance of Pūjā for Devas/Kuladevatas

Crowdsource FAQs for these topics & put up good videos in English & at least two Deśabhāṣa-s.

Hold in-person weekly classes for 30-40 kids & livestream them. Use Svasampradāya & Bhakti lore to anchor the kids in the basic tenets.

“Strīdharma is important because this female Nāyanār, who is like a mother to us, upheld it”

“Due to the alpa puṇya committed as a spider, it was born as Nāyanār Koceṅgat-Cōzhan.”

And so on.

Current non-attachment

Source: TW

I agree that the average Hindu is presently not attached to any Sampradāya. But that may not be necessarily an inherent flaw but due to kālapratāpa, lack of jñāna and societal factors suppressing an otherwise natural capacity to be devoted to a Sampradāya.

How can you know that you want to commit to this Sampradāya if you are given no opportunity to learn about its existence?

Maybe if he or she came to have a bit of appreciation of a Sampradāya’s beauty and depth, he or she can decide “this is the Sampradāya for me. I want to commit myself to this rich path and these blessed Pūrvācāryas”. So, each Sampradāya must put some effort to make simple but accurate content accessible to potential initiates and seekers of any H background. +++(4)+++

There may be hundreds, if not thousands, of H of a non-typical background who may be sub-consciously yearning for a Saiddhāntika, Śrīvidyā, Mādhva, Śrī-Vaiṣṇava or pure pāñcarātrika path but there are levels of obstacles in their paths.

Hurdles

They may be taking an indefinitely long time to come across these things due to being from a non-typical background. When they do, they struggle because nobody is too interested in explaining anything to them in a jargon-free language that they can relate to and understand— not out of malice but the urge to propagate one’s own Sampradāya is simply missing. Being excessively guarded doesn’t help as well.

Awaiting desire

Also, most Sampradāyas don’t cater to a very important class: those who are not initiated or deem themselves not ready for initiation but are genuinely devoted to the Sampradāya and are happy to be part of it in some way while they wait for a desire for deeper commitment to enter them.

In this regard, the Siddhānta has carved out a space for such a class, which it calls “Rudrāṃśa” or ”Māheśvara”, including lay devotional activities and the express Āgamika provision of last rites for members of such a class which are conducted by Saiddhāntika Ācāryas for them in a special way.

Challenges

Source: TW

Different Sampradāyas have different challenges but broadly:

Endogamy game

Most Sat-Sampradāyas are at high risk of degenerating into merely endogamous communities with no vision for a Pan-H following. This is a travesty and a degeneration and must be recognized as such. Your Sat-Sampradāya should first and foremost be about the Pūrvācāryas and the Siddhānta they established. An endogamous community for whom the Sampradāya is nothing more than life-cycle rites and some communal festivals will amount to nothing much at the end of the day.

Orthopraxy overrating

Orthopraxy’s standalone importance is over-rated. Yes, rituals are the body of the Sampradāya but faith in them is the soul and Tattvajñāna is the food which keeps that soul in the body.

“It’s ok to do without understanding the meaning or significance” is a degenerate idea that should be abandoned. It’s perfectly okay to know only a little and not know everything but it’s not okay to think that one does not have to strive to understand the significance of what one does.

Those whose practice is rooted in “let me just get through this observance for the sake of my family” will never be capable of passing down anything except an increasingly malnourished body of practices that will eventually die because the soul (faith) has left the body.

sat-sampradAya-s

My idea of Sat-Sampradāyas is quite broad: Siddhānta (with 4/5 divisions), Pāśupata, Vīraśaiva/Śrautaśaiva, Nātha, Trika/Krama, Śrīkula, Kālīkula, Gauḍiya, Mādhva, Rāmānuja, Nimbārka, Vallabha, Vaikhānasa, etc.

विस्तारः (द्रष्टुं नोद्यम्)

I was mainly trying to recall all Āgama/Tantra-based Sampradāyas; so was busy listing these. I was not trying to include smārta-Sampradāya since it’s a common base for all. Plus, in retrospect, I thought I had done the job by including Śrīkula/Śrīvidyā.

Each of these offers a complete system consisting of both darśana and upāsana. I have learnt quite a bit about Mādhva doctrines from you and @JoraShankara .

I think it’s important that each Sampradāya puts itself out there for genuine seekers, anchoring them deep in Hindudharma while publicly avowing a constructive stand as regards fellow Sat-Sampradāyas.

I honestly think there is a limited window for Sat-Sampradāyas to make known their relevance and importance. The hard truth is that our numerical superiority is not of much use because the average Hindu is not grounded in his faith; his decisions and values are not informed by his faith. This is where Sat-Sampradāyas need to come in, and they ought to have started this aggressively at least a few decades earlier. But it’s not too late yet and they ought to take this seriously.
