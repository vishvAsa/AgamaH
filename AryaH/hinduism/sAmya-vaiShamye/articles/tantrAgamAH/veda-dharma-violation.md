## Common impression
> The only real tension these Tantric thinkers had with the Vedic tradition concerned the rejection of what we would call today the primacy of Smārta orthopraxy - that is, the social and legal framework represented by the Dharmaśāstras, that becomes the reference for which eventually became the standard by which many people define what counts as “orthodoxly Vedic” - something that inevitably passes through the systematizations of Pūrvamīmāṁsā and Uttaramīmāṁsā, both of which opposed Bhartṛhari’s Vyākaraṇa in various ways.   
> That is precisely why I insist so strongly on going beyond those later frameworks and recovering an older layer of Vedic thought through Bhartṛhari.

## Cosmic order
"later" dharmashAstra is based on veda only. Do-s and don't-s (pretty harsh ones too) are right there in the saMhita-s. "वेदोऽखिलो धर्ममूलम्" said father manu and others. 

And, good tantra (shaiva and vaiShNava) never violates the cosmic and social order expressed in vedas. The transgressive tantra does, partly due to bad theology - if you are absolutely identical to God and you know it, rules don't apply to you.

## Getting high
Another source of degeneracy among vAma-tANtrika-s is their heavy focus on getting high (whether spiritually or chemically induced), rather than feeling joy via uniting with the cosmic order, which is not whimsy but mathematical in nature. The latter is found in actual veda-s and emphasized in good tantras.

## Origin difference
The difference is reflected in the social context where these systems originated. The steppe, where vedas first resounded, is a very harsh place. The Aryas won Eurasia because of dharma. vAma-tantra emerged in a more decadent context - (not coincidentally) just before barbarian invasions.
