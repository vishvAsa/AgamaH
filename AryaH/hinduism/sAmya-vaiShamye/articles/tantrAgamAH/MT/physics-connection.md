In a general sense, I do think that aspects of tAntrika thought/philosophy have good qualitative analogies in various facets of the modern understanding of physical existence and its interface with the less understood (within the modern framework) aspects of reality like consciousness. 

One Occidental author, Muller-Ortega, while disparaged by the Sandersonian school as a bad scholar, has actually put such a view in print. IMO, he correctly argues that the analogies derived from the work of the philosophical physicist David Bohm (student of Robert Oppenheimer) in his "Wholeness and the Implicate Order" are useful in understanding abhinavagupta's account of the nature of the bhairava in the tantrAloka (which is also related to the verse of ratnAkara cited in the thread).  

Hence, I'm all for using modern physical and other analogies in the interpretative framework of ancient texts in private teaching and exposition. However, I mostly refrain from such in public for each has to develop their own understanding of the text from its literal form.
