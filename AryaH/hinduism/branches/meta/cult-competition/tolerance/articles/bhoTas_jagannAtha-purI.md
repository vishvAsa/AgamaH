18th c. CE Tibetan Bauddhācārya on Jagannātha
('Jigs-med-gling-pa's IHophyogs rgya-gar-gyi gtam)

If one goes for about fifteen days in a direction south-west of Kālighāṭa, there is a mahān caitya (mchod-rten chen-po) called Jagannātha (Dzo-ka-na, in Purī), which can be seen from a distance of three days' journey. The pradhāna bali (mchod-pa'i gtso-bo) made there is the gaṇacakra (tshogs-'khor) but the Phiraṅga (Phereng-pa) i.e. Europeans, and the Turuṣkas (Tu-rub), i.e. Muslims, and others are varjita to do nivedana there because it is said: "[Those] of duṣṭa vaṁsa [are] different [from us], but the Bhoṭas [Bhoṭa], i.e. Tibetans, and us are of eka vaṁsa". So they do not obstruct [the Bhoṭas from doing nivedana]. If one considers this, then I think it is in accordance with the kathā (gtam-rgyud) of Rājā Rūpati (Ru-pa-ti).

~ Tibetan Bauddhācārya Jigme Lingpa (1730-1798)
from rNying-ma-pa (Nyingma paramparā)

So, on basis of this account,Tibetans, unlike other mlecchas, weren't restricted from Jagannātha mandira praveśa in 18th c. CE
