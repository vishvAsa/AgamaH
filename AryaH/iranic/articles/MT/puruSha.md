Iranian "Puruṣa-sūkta"  

The Sāmānid official Aḥmad al-Jayhānī, while a Mohammedan, was suspected by some of his co-religionists to be a cryptic Manichaeist. He preserves an interesting Iranic fragment that we, as practitioners of a sister religion, can immediately see cognacy with. 

The god Ahura Mazdā says:

> "I created the whole of this world from myself. I created the souls of the righteous from the hair of my head; the sky from my brain; nails and arms (?) from my forehead; the sun from my eye; the moon from my nose; the stars from my tongue; Sraoša and the rest of the Ameša Spenta-s from my ear; the earth from the tendon of my foot."

Here the macranthropic puruṣa is applied to Ahura Mazdā by the Iranians. 

A similar concept seems to have been transmitted by the early Eastern Iranians to the Cīna-s, who incorporated it as-is in their origin mythology. 

It is also notable that of the Ameša Spenta-s or yazata-s, the one deity mentioned by name is Sraoša. Sraoša was particularly popular in the Eastern Iranian reflex and syncretized with Skanda for the Hindu votaries. It is quite likely that the Śaiva shrines in what is today Dilberjin were also frequented by Iranics who identified Skanda with Sraoša of Kṣathra Vairya.
